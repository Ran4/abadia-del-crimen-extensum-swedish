### 1.2 How each unit was reviewed

- **Translation** (`translate:<unit>`): a translator worked from the Spanish, using en/fr/it only for meaning, and followed
  the glossary.
- **Each round** ran two passes in parallel:
  - **Editor** (`edit:<unit>:rN`): a Swedish editor read the Swedish against the Spanish. It checked
    correctness, idiom, register, glossary use, forms of address, length and paging, and the markers.
  - **Blind back-translation** (`backtr:<unit>:rN`), then a **meaning check** (`meaning:<unit>:rN`). A separate agent
    saw only the Swedish lines, with no files, no commands and no web. It translated them literally into
    English, marking NI and DU, and listed whatever read as unclear. The meaning checker compared that
    back-translation with the Spanish (en/fr/it where useful). It looked for omissions, additions, changed facts,
    shifts in tone or address, and lost player information, and it judged every segment flagged as unclear.
- **Revision** (`revise:<unit>:rN`): if either pass had a must or should, a reviser fixed each right issue,
  rejected each wrong one with a reason, and the next round began. The next round's reviewers saw those
  rejections and could raise an issue again if the reason did not hold.
- **Nits pass** (`nits:<unit>`): when a round had no must or should, this last pass applied the nits it
  agreed with and rejected the others with a reason. The limit was 4 rounds.
- **Severity:** *must* means wrong: ungrammatical, wrong meaning, a broken rule or glossary form,
  wrong address, or lost player information. *should* means clearly unidiomatic, stiff or off-register,
  a noticeable shift in nuance, or a poor page cut. *nit* means a small preference.
- **Deviation from the brief:** the brief asks for a back-translation into Spanish. The
  back-translator wrote literal English instead, and the meaning checker compared that with the Spanish.
